## Activation & Operations

### Activation Criteria

#### Temperature-Based Triggers
- **Primary Threshold**: -5°C or below (with wind chill)
- **Extended Cold Snap**: Multi-day forecast below 0°C
- **Wind Chill Warning**: Environment Canada alerts
- **Extreme Weather**: Blizzards, ice storms, or severe conditions
- **Community Request**: Local emergency services recommendation

**Activation Decision Tool**:
```
Current Temperature: _____ °C
Wind Chill Factor: _____ °C
Weather Forecast (48h): _______________
Special Circumstances: _______________

☐ Automatic Activation Required (Temp < -5°C)
☐ Discretionary Activation (0 to -5°C with factors)
☐ Pre-Activation Planning (Cold snap approaching)
☐ No Activation Required
```

#### Activation Process

**Step 1: Decision (By 2:00 PM)**
- Review weather forecast
- Check facility availability
- Confirm volunteer coverage
- Verify supply inventory

**Step 2: Notification (By 3:00 PM)**
- Alert volunteer coordinator
- Notify confirmed volunteers
- Update status system (warming_room_status.py)
- Post to social media
- Contact local media outlets
- Inform emergency services
- Update 211 Ontario database

**Step 3: Preparation (3:00 PM - 6:00 PM)**
- Unlock and secure facility
- Turn on heating systems
- Set up sleeping areas
- Prepare meal service
- Stock supplies
- Test safety equipment
- Brief volunteers

**Step 4: Opening (6:00 PM)**
- Official opening
- Guest check-in begins
- Services commence
- Monitor capacity

**Activation Checklist**:
```
☐ Weather reviewed and documented
☐ Facility access secured
☐ Volunteers confirmed (minimum 3)
☐ Heating system operational
☐ Supplies inventoried and sufficient
☐ Safety equipment checked
☐ Notifications sent (all channels)
☐ Emergency contacts verified
☐ Opening time posted publicly
☐ Incident reports ready
```

### Deactivation Process

**Criteria for Closing**:
- Temperature forecast above 0°C
- Weather event concluded
- Facility no longer available
- Emergency situation requiring closure

**Deactivation Steps**:
1. Announce closing time (minimum 2 hours notice)
2. Assist guests with alternative arrangements
3. Conduct facility walkthrough
4. Secure all exits and utilities
5. Complete closing documentation
6. Update status systems
7. Notify stakeholders
8. Return keys/secure building

**Closing Checklist**:
```
☐ All guests departed with safe alternatives
☐ Facility cleaned and sanitized
☐ Supplies inventoried and stored
☐ Heating/cooling systems adjusted
☐ Lights off, doors locked
☐ Security system armed (if applicable)
☐ Keys returned to designated holder
☐ Closing report completed
☐ Status system updated
☐ Public notification issued
```

## Facility Requirements

### Ideal Location Characteristics

#### Essential Features
- **Accessibility**: Ground floor or elevator access
- **Capacity**: Minimum 20-30 people
- **Heating**: Reliable, sufficient heating system
- **Washrooms**: Gender-inclusive facilities with multiple stalls
- **Water**: Running water and sink access
- **Kitchen**: Basic meal preparation capability
- **Emergency Exits**: Multiple clearly marked exits
- **Parking**: Nearby parking for volunteers and emergency vehicles
- **Public Transit**: Within walking distance of bus routes
- **Central Location**: Accessible to target population

#### Desirable Features
- **Shower Facilities**: Personal hygiene support
- **Laundry**: Washer and dryer access
- **Privacy Areas**: Spaces for confidential conversations
- **Storage**: Secure storage for supplies and belongings
- **Outdoor Space**: Designated smoking area
- **Security System**: Cameras and alarm system
- **Wi-Fi**: Internet access for guests and staff
- **Charging Stations**: Device charging capability
- **Quiet Rooms**: Space for those needing low-stimulation environment

### Space Planning

#### Sleeping Area (Primary Space)
- **Spacing**: Minimum 3 feet between mats/cots
- **Layout**: Organized sections (men's, women's, families)
- **Lighting**: Dimmable lights for overnight
- **Temperature**: Maintain 18-22°C
- **Ventilation**: Fresh air circulation

**Capacity Calculation**:
```
Square Footage: _____ sq ft
÷ 35 sq ft per person = _____ maximum capacity
Safety Factor (0.8) = _____ operational capacity
```

#### Common Areas
- **Registration Desk**: Check-in/check-out station
- **Waiting Area**: Comfortable seating
- **Meal Service**: Tables and chairs
- **Activity Space**: Recreation and relaxation
- **Medical Area**: Private space for health needs

#### Support Spaces
- **Volunteer Room**: Break area for staff
- **Storage**: Supplies, equipment, donations
- **Office**: Administrative work and phone calls
- **Outdoor Smoking Area**: Designated safe space

### Naloxone Protocol

**Opioid Overdose Signs**:
- Unresponsive or unconscious
- Slow/no breathing
- Blue lips or fingernails
- Pinpoint pupils
- Choking/gurgling sounds

**Response Steps**:
1. Call 911 immediately
2. Administer naloxone (nasal spray or injection)
3. Perform rescue breathing if trained
4. Place in recovery position
5. Stay with person until help arrives
6. Second dose if no response in 3-5 minutes
7. Document incident

**Good Samaritan Law**: Protection for those calling 911 for overdoses in Ontario

## Best Practices & Lessons Learned

### Operational Best Practices

**1. Start Small, Scale Gradually**
- Begin with limited nights (coldest weather only)
- Expand as capacity and experience grow
- Learn from each activation

**2. Prioritize Safety**
- Never compromise on safety protocols
- Train all volunteers thoroughly
- Maintain emergency equipment
- Practice drills regularly

**3. Build Community Partnerships**
- Engage stakeholders early
- Share successes and challenges
- Collaborate rather than compete
- Leverage collective resources

**4. Focus on Dignity**
- Treat guests with respect
- Minimize barriers to access
- Offer choices when possible
- Celebrate individual strengths

**5. Document Everything**
- Keep detailed records
- Learn from incidents
- Track outcomes
- Share data appropriately

**6. Support Your Team**
- Prevent volunteer burnout
- Provide adequate training
- Offer debriefing opportunities
- Recognize contributions

### Common Challenges & Solutions

**Challenge**: Last-minute volunteer cancellations
**Solution**: Maintain on-call backup list, cross-train volunteers, offer incentives for flexibility

**Challenge**: Behavioral issues and conflicts
**Solution**: Clear rules, consistent enforcement, de-escalation training, professional support available

**Challenge**: Funding sustainability
**Solution**: Diversify funding sources, demonstrate impact, build community support, create giving campaigns

**Challenge**: Neighborhood opposition
**Solution**: Community consultation, address concerns proactively, share data, involve neighbors

**Challenge**: Guest needs exceeding capacity
**Solution**: Develop overflow protocols, create waitlist management, partner with other services, expand gradually

### Success Stories Framework

**Document Impact**:
- Individual success stories (with permission)
- Community testimonials
- Volunteer experiences
- Partnership highlights
- Measurable outcomes

**Share Appropriately**:
- Protect privacy and anonymity
- Focus on strengths and dignity
- Avoid exploitation
- Celebrate collective success

## Conclusion

This comprehensive toolkit provides the essential frameworks, checklists, and resources needed to successfully establish and operate warming room facilities. Successful implementation requires:

1. **Strong Leadership**: Dedicated coordinators with vision and follow-through
2. **Community Support**: Broad-based engagement and partnership
3. **Adequate Resources**: Funding, supplies, and facilities
4. **Trained Volunteers**: Compassionate, skilled, and committed individuals
5. **Safety Focus**: Protocols that protect both guests and volunteers
6. **Continuous Improvement**: Learning from experience and adapting
7. **Person-Centered Approach**: Services that respect dignity and autonomy

By following this toolkit and adapting it to local needs, communities can create warming room operations that save lives, provide dignity, and connect vulnerable individuals with pathways to stability and housing.
