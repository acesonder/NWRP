# Proposal: Volunteer Coordination System

**Author:** Example Contributor  
**Date:** 2025-10-20  
**Status:** Draft

## Summary

Implement a structured volunteer coordination system to ensure consistent staffing coverage during warming room operations, including scheduling, training, and communication protocols.

## Problem Statement

During warming room activations, coordinating volunteers can be chaotic without a centralized system. This leads to:
- Gaps in coverage when volunteers don't show up
- Confusion about shift responsibilities
- Inconsistent service delivery
- Volunteer burnout from unclear expectations

## Proposed Solution

Create a three-tiered volunteer coordination system:

### 1. Volunteer Database
- Maintain a list of trained, approved volunteers
- Track availability and preferences
- Record skills and qualifications (first aid, languages spoken, etc.)

### 2. Scheduling System
- Create clear shift schedules (e.g., 6pm-10pm, 10pm-2am, 2am-6am)
- Define minimum staffing requirements per shift
- Implement a backup system for no-shows
- Send automated reminders 24 hours before shifts

### 3. Communication Protocol
- Primary contact method (phone/text/email)
- Group messaging for urgent updates
- Check-in system at start of shifts
- Incident reporting process

## Benefits

- **Reliability**: Guaranteed coverage with backup volunteers
- **Efficiency**: Clear expectations reduce confusion and stress
- **Scalability**: Easy to onboard and train new volunteers
- **Accountability**: Track volunteer hours and contributions
- **Safety**: Ensure proper staffing ratios for guest safety

## Challenges & Risks

### Challenge 1: Technology Access
Not all volunteers may have smartphones or reliable internet access.
- **Mitigation**: Offer multiple communication methods (phone calls, text, email)

### Challenge 2: Last-Minute Cancellations
Volunteers may cancel at the last minute due to emergencies.
- **Mitigation**: Maintain a pool of on-call backup volunteers

### Challenge 3: Training Consistency
Ensuring all volunteers receive proper training.
- **Mitigation**: Develop standardized training materials and required orientation

## Resources Required

### Personnel
- **Volunteer Coordinator**: Part-time role to manage system (10-15 hrs/week)
- **Training Lead**: Develop and deliver volunteer training
- **Shift Leads**: Experienced volunteers to supervise each shift

### Materials/Equipment
- Volunteer management software or spreadsheet system
- Training materials (handbook, videos)
- Volunteer ID badges
- Emergency contact cards

### Financial
- Volunteer management software: $0-500/year (depending on solution)
- Training materials: $200
- Volunteer recognition/appreciation: $500/year
- **Total estimated**: $700-1,200/year

## Implementation Timeline

### Phase 1: Planning (Weeks 1-2)
- Define volunteer roles and shift structure
- Create volunteer application form
- Develop training curriculum
- Select scheduling system/tool

### Phase 2: Recruitment & Training (Weeks 3-6)
- Recruit initial volunteer coordinator
- Post volunteer opportunities
- Conduct background checks
- Complete initial training sessions

### Phase 3: Pilot (Weeks 7-10)
- Test system during next warming room activation
- Gather feedback from volunteers and guests
- Refine processes based on learnings

### Phase 4: Full Implementation (Week 11+)
- Expand volunteer pool
- Implement automated scheduling and reminders
- Establish regular coordinator meetings

## Success Metrics

- **Coverage Rate**: 95%+ of shifts filled with confirmed volunteers
- **No-Show Rate**: Less than 10% of scheduled shifts
- **Volunteer Retention**: 70%+ of volunteers return for multiple activations
- **Guest Satisfaction**: Positive feedback on consistent service
- **Response Time**: All shifts filled within 24 hours of activation notice
